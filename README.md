# Maasai IPR Protection Initiative
## Enkop Culture - Community Empowerment Website

### 🌍 Website Vision

**Purpose:** To protect Maasai culture, products, and knowledge by explaining IPR in simple, community-friendly language.

**This is NOT a legal website. This IS a learning + empowerment website.**

---

### 🎯 What Visitors Should Feel

1. **Pride** - in Maasai culture and heritage
2. **Trust** - in the information and guidance provided
3. **Clarity** - understanding what IPR is and how to use it

---

### 📋 Website Structure

#### 1. Home Page - Strong First Impression
- **Hero Section:** "Protecting Maasai Products, Knowledge & Culture"
- **Why This Matters:** Culture is copied, community must benefit, IPR is a protection tool
- **Quick Navigation Tiles:** Direct access to each IPR type and Enkare case study

#### 2. About the Initiative
- Story-based explanation (not academic)
- Who are the Maasai?
- Why protection is needed
- Importance of community ownership (Enkop)
- Respect for elders & tradition

#### 3. IPR Basics - Core Learning Section
Each IPR type has detailed information:
- **Trademark (™)** - Protect names & logos
- **Geographical Indication (GI)** - Protect place-based products
- **Design** - Protect visual patterns
- **Copyright (©)** - Protect stories & music
- **Patent** - Protect new processes

**For Each IPR:**
- What is it? (Simple words)
- What it protects
- What it does NOT protect
- Who should own it (community)
- Simple application process
- Maa keywords
- Example from Maasai life

#### 4. Maasai Products & Protection
Helps identify which IPR applies to which product:
- **Beadwork** → GI + Design + Trademark
- **Shúkà** → GI + Design
- **Leather Products** → Design + Trademark
- **Herbal Products** → Patent (process) + Trademark
- **Dance & Stories** → Copyright

#### 5. Case Study: Enkare Water
Real, practical example showing:
- What is Enkare?
- Why water cannot be owned (but the NAME can be protected)
- Trademark process step-by-step
- Community ownership model
- Lessons for other communities

#### 6. Community Ownership (Enkop)
- What is community ownership?
- Why individuals should not own culture
- Role of elders, women, youth
- Benefit sharing
- Examples from Maasai life (grazing land, water sharing)

#### 7. Action Plan - What to Do Next
Practical step-by-step guide:
1. Form a cooperative
2. List products
3. Classify products
4. Choose correct IPR
5. Apply for protection
6. Monitor misuse

Includes downloadable checklist (coming soon)

#### 8. Resources & Help
- Links to Kenya IPR offices (KIPI)
- Tanzania BRELA
- WIPO resources
- NGO partners
- Contact information

- **Simple & Clear** - Large fonts, icons instead of heavy text
- **Mobile-First** - Most users will access on phones
- **Community Focus** - Use "we" instead of "they"
- **Story-Based** - Examples instead of definitions
- **Accessible** - Multiple languages (English + Maa keywords)
